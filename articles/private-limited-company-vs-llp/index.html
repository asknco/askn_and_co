Private Limited Company or LLP: Which Suits Your Business?

By ASKN & Co. LLP · 15 July 2025 · 4 min read

Founders in India usually choose between two structures with limited liability: the private limited company and the limited liability partnership (LLP). Both protect personal assets from business debts, and both are registered online with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. They differ sharply in tax, compliance and, above all, in how easily they can raise money.

Side-by-side comparison

Governing law

  • Private limited company: Companies Act, 2013
  • LLP: Limited Liability Partnership Act, 2008

Minimum members

  • Company: two directors and two shareholders (can be the same people)
  • LLP: two designated partners

Liability

  • Both: limited to the capital contributed, except in cases of fraud

Raising equity investment

  • Company: shares can be issued to investors; this is the structure venture capital and angel investors expect
  • LLP: investors can only come in as partners, which most institutional investors will not do

ESOPs

  • Company: can issue employee stock options
  • LLP: cannot issue ESOPs

Statutory audit

  • Company: mandatory every year, whatever the turnover
  • LLP: required only if turnover exceeds ₹40 lakh or contribution exceeds ₹25 lakh

Annual compliance

  • Company: board meetings, AGM, AOC-4, MGT-7 or MGT-7A, DIR-3 KYC and statutory registers
  • LLP: Form 8 (statement of accounts and solvency) and Form 11 (annual return), with far fewer meetings and registers

Tax comparison

  • Company: a domestic company that opts for section 115BAA pays tax at 22% plus a 10% surcharge and 4% cess — an effective rate of about 25.17%, without most deductions and incentives. Profits distributed as dividends are taxed again in the shareholders' hands at their slab rates.
  • LLP: taxed at 30% plus surcharge (where income exceeds ₹1 crore) and cess. The partners' share of profit is exempt in their hands, and interest on capital and remuneration to working partners are deductible within the limits of section 40(b).

For a profitable business that distributes most of its profits to its owners, an LLP can result in a lower combined tax. For a business that retains profits to grow, the company's lower rate is usually better.

Foreign investment

Foreign direct investment is permitted in both, but more simply in companies. LLPs can receive FDI under the automatic route only in sectors where 100% FDI is allowed automatically without performance-linked conditions.

Which should you choose?

Choose a private limited company if:

  • You plan to raise angel, venture capital or private equity funding
  • You want to offer ESOPs to attract employees
  • You are building a startup and want DPIIT recognition and its benefits
  • Customers, banks or government tenders expect a company

Choose an LLP if:

  • The business is a professional practice, consultancy or family business
  • You do not intend to bring in outside equity investors
  • You want lower compliance cost and fewer formalities
  • Profits will largely be distributed to the owners

Can you change later?

Yes. An LLP can be converted into a company, and a company can be converted into an LLP, subject to conditions. Conversion takes time and professional cost, and tax neutrality depends on meeting specific conditions — so it is better to choose correctly at the start.

Frequently asked questions

Which is better for a startup, a private limited company or an LLP?

A private limited company, if you plan to raise equity investment, issue ESOPs or seek DPIIT startup recognition. An LLP cannot issue shares or ESOPs, which rules it out for most funded startups.

Is an LLP cheaper to maintain than a private limited company?

Generally, yes. An LLP needs an audit only if its turnover exceeds ₹40 lakh or contribution exceeds ₹25 lakh, and its annual filings are limited to Form 8 and Form 11. A company needs an audit every year and has more meetings, registers and ROC forms.

Which pays less tax, an LLP or a private limited company?

A company opting for section 115BAA pays about 25.17%, but dividends are taxed again in shareholders' hands. An LLP pays 30% plus surcharge and cess, and the partners' share of profit is exempt. If most profits are distributed, an LLP often costs less overall; if profits are retained, a company usually does.

Can an LLP be converted into a private limited company later?

Yes, subject to conditions under company law, and tax neutrality depends on meeting specific conditions. Conversion takes time and cost, so it is better to choose the right structure at the start.
